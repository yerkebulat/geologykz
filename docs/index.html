Context

Geology of Kazakhstan

Kazakhstan sits at the heart of Eurasia, where ancient Precambrian microcontinents were stitched together by the Paleozoic Central Asian Orogenic Belt. That assembly produced the distinctive Kazakhstan orocline — a vast horseshoe of folded belts wrapping around the older cores of the Chu–Ili, Kokchetav, and other terranes.

To the west, the Caspian basin holds thick Mesozoic–Cenozoic sedimentary successions above a deeper Paleozoic foundation. In central Kazakhstan, Carboniferous sedimentary sequences host the world-class Zhezkazgan sedimentary copper deposits. Farther east and southeast, Devonian–Carboniferous magmatic arcs host major porphyry copper systems such as Kounrad and Aktogay.

The layers shown here are lithology polygons for the Zhezkazgan and Mangistau regions, drawn from the Orr and Associates (2006) compilation. They are a first window onto the geology; the long-term aim is a seamless national map.
